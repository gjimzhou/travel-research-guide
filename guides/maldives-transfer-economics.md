# Maldives Split-Stay and Transfer Logic

Prior Maldives discussions compared high-end resorts including Waldorf Astoria Maldives Ithaafushi, Park Hyatt Maldives Hadahaa, and The St. Regis Maldives Vommuli Resort.

The recurring planning issue was whether combining multiple resorts improves the trip enough to justify the extra transfer.

## Transfer friction is part of the hotel comparison

A Maldives resort should not be compared on room rate alone.

The practical comparison also includes:

- seaplane, boat, yacht, or domestic-air transfer structure;
- transfer time;
- transfer cost;
- whether arrival or departure timing creates extra friction;
- how much of a day is lost when changing resorts.

## Split stays need enough time

A split stay makes the most sense when:

- the overall Maldives trip is long enough;
- the two resorts offer materially different experiences;
- the transfer does not consume too much of the usable trip.

The previously discussed 5+5 style structure is much more defensible than repeatedly moving after only a few nights.

## Why the resorts can justify a split

The comparison was not simply brand prestige. The relevant differences included:

- resort scale;
- remoteness;
- reef/snorkeling environment;
- villa style;
- dining;
- loyalty/points economics;
- overall atmosphere.

A second resort should add a different experience, not just another luxury room.

## General conclusion

In the Maldives, the transfer itself is part of the product. A theoretically better hotel can create a worse trip if the move consumes too much time or adds too much friction.
