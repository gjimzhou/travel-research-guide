# Travel Planning Framework

## 1. Minimize unnecessary hotel churn

A high-quality hotel can be part of the destination. Moving every one or two nights often destroys the value of resort amenities and adds repeated packing, check-in, and transfer costs.

## 2. Optimize the itinerary as a system

Evaluate together:

- airfare and ground, boat, or inter-island transfers;
- hotel cancellation deadlines;
- resort credits and elite benefits;
- parking and rental-car costs;
- golf or activity availability;
- weather and maintenance risk;
- restaurant timing;
- local sunset where relevant.

## 3. Separate confirmed facts from research state

Useful labels include:

- current published fact;
- historical price;
- quoted policy;
- considered option;
- needs re-verification.

This prevents an old rate, screenshot, or concierge answer from later being mistaken for a current fact.

## 4. Value credits by avoided spend

A nominal resort credit is worth less than face value if it induces spending that would not otherwise occur. Credits are most valuable when they replace planned breakfast, dining, spa, golf, or transportation spend.

## 5. Work backward from sunset

For golf, photography, beach dinners, scenic drives, and boat transfers, use local sunset and realistic duration rather than generic labels such as "afternoon."

## 6. Price logistics, not just rooms

A hotel that is modestly cheaper can still produce a more expensive or less enjoyable trip if it adds long transfers, parking charges, resort fees, or repeated taxis.

## 7. Re-plan after a major disruption

When weather, closures, or transport failures affect a core component, reconsider the entire itinerary instead of mechanically replacing only the failed item.
