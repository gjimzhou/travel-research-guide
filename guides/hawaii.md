# Hawaii Luxury-Travel Research

## Big Island resort archetypes

### Kona Village, A Rosewood Resort

Kona Village is differentiated by detached hale accommodations, low-density design, lava-coast setting, and a strong sense of place.

Rosewood describes 150 stand-alone guest hale and emphasizes oversized lanais, outdoor showers, ocean activities, cultural programming, and multiple accommodation orientations.

The practical implication is that **room category and location are part of the resort choice**. Beachfront, oceanfront, tree-top, lagoon, and multi-bedroom products can create materially different stays.

### Four Seasons Resort Hualālai

Hualālai is especially strong as an integrated luxury resort: golf, multiple pools, spa/fitness, dining, marine programming, and other on-property activities can support a full stay with little logistical overhead.

Four Seasons currently lists eight outdoor pools and a Jack Nicklaus Signature golf course on property.

### Mauna Lani, Auberge Resorts Collection

Mauna Lani provides a different design and activity mix and is useful in comparisons where golf access, cultural programming, and the broader Kohala Coast setting matter as much as the room itself.

## Multi-resort design

Changing resorts can make sense when each property contributes a genuinely different experience.

A useful test:

**Would the itinerary still include both properties if they were next door to each other?**

If yes, the split may be about product differentiation. If no, the split may simply be adding movement.

## Room-category selection

At a destination resort, compare the exact room product rather than the hotel logo.

Questions include:

- direct beach access or only ocean view?
- detached unit or main building?
- sunrise or sunset orientation?
- private plunge pool?
- distance to pools/restaurants?
- outdoor shower / lanai value?
- privacy vs. convenience?

## Operational changes

Luxury resorts should be rechecked close to travel because individual facilities can close without the whole hotel closing.

Example: Four Seasons Hualālai currently states that Beach Tree Pool & Deck is temporarily closed beginning September 8, 2026, with reopening expected in mid-November; its other pool facilities remain available.

The general lesson is not that the resort is “closed.” It is that amenity-level maintenance can change the value of a specific stay.

## Weather and island selection

When a storm materially affects one island or region, reevaluate the whole itinerary rather than only the disrupted activity.

Check:

- roads;
- airports;
- golf-course operations;
- power restoration;
- beach/ocean conditions;
- hotel operations;
- cancellation deadlines.

## General lesson

A luxury Hawaii itinerary should optimize the system of hotel experience, room category, driving time, golf/activities, restaurant timing, cancellation policy, and weather resilience—not maximize island or resort count.
