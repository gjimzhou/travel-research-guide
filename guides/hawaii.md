# Hawaii Luxury-Travel Research

## Big Island resort positioning

### Rosewood Kona Village

Kona Village is differentiated by detached-hale accommodations, low-density design, lava-coast setting, and a strong sense of place. It is well suited to travelers who value resort atmosphere and architectural identity over a conventional tower-resort format.

### Four Seasons Resort Hualālai

Hualālai is especially strong as an integrated luxury resort: golf, pools, dining, spa, service, and on-property activities can support a full stay without much logistical overhead.

### Mauna Lani, Auberge Resorts Collection

Mauna Lani provides a different design language and strong access to golf and cultural programming, making it useful in comparisons where resort character matters as much as beach quality.

## Multi-resort design

On the Big Island, changing resorts can make sense when each property contributes a meaningfully different experience. It becomes less attractive when hotel changes merely add packing and driving without adding a distinct setting or activity set.

## Weather and island selection

When a storm materially affects one island, reevaluate the whole itinerary rather than only the disrupted activity. Golf-course closures, road conditions, power restoration, hotel operations, and cancellation deadlines can interact.

## General lesson

A luxury Hawaii itinerary should optimize the system of hotel experience, driving time, golf/activities, restaurant timing, cancellation policy, and weather resilience—not maximize the number of islands or resorts.
